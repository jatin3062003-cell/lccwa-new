Academic Program

Our Academic Curriculum & Operations

We run throughout the year and conduct daily teaching sessions for 3rd - 12th grade[cite: 909]. We give our children a month-long vacation in the month of May[cite: 910]. We teach students from Maharashtra State Board, English, Marathi, Hindi and Semi-English medium[cite: 911].

Junior College Admissions & Testing

Admissions for 11th std start shortly after 10th board results are announced[cite: 912]. We conduct selection tests for students who wish to take up Science to ensure that they will be able to cope with the syllabus[cite: 913]. Students from Arts and Commerce are also tested for their Maths skills before taking up Maths as a subject in junior college[cite: 914].

Our full-time teachers are ably supported by an excellent team of dedicated volunteers who conduct classes with our students on a regular basis[cite: 915]. The volunteers are mostly from the Powai community while some are from the IIT Bombay NSS group[cite: 916]. We could not have done justice to the wide variety of subjects and grades being taught at LCCWA without the crucial support of our volunteers and we are truly appreciative of their active involvement through the pandemic despite any challenges they may have faced[cite: 917].

Infrastructure & Aids

By the generosity of our donors, we have recently acquired 29 state of the art desktop computers on which the children from class 5 to class 10 learn the basics of Computer usage[cite: 918]. All our classrooms are wifi enabled and have LED TVs for use as teaching aids[cite: 919].

Board Results

Problem Statement

The Reality of Mumbai's Low-Income Areas

Children living in Mumbai’s low-income areas lack the basic facilities needed for them to learn, grow and become responsible citizens contributing to the progress of the nation[cite: 922]. With almost a quarter of all children in Mumbai living in these conditions, the future of the city will likely be defined by how it takes care of their development[cite: 923].

The COVID-19 pandemic has devastated the lives of many underprivileged families[cite: 924]. The already challenging environment of these children has deteriorated further, in some cases due to loss of a family member, key earning member and in many cases loss of a key source of livelihood[cite: 924].

Background of Beneficiaries

Children living in the bustees of Powai, like any other, come from extremely appalling socio-economic situations[cite: 925]. Their parents mostly work as construction workers, house-maids, auto-rickshaw drivers, labourers or road-side vendors[cite: 926].

Some are supported by single mothers deserted by their fathers, some have migrated from villages in search of a better life, some are suffering because of their parents’ alcohol addiction, and it is not uncommon to have family returning from jail after spending time behind bars[cite: 927]. What all of them have in common is that they have minimal resources and an uncertain future[cite: 928]. Some of the children have lost their family members to COVID and a large number of their parents have lost their livelihood due to the pandemic[cite: 929].

Challenges Beyond Academics

While academic support is crucial, children from underprivileged backgrounds face several interconnected challenges that impact their overall development[cite: 930]:

01

Health, Nutrition & Hygiene

Inadequate nutrition and poor health and hygiene practices—especially menstrual hygiene awareness for girls—often affect children’s ability to concentrate and perform well in school[cite: 930].

02

Limited Exposure & Skills

Lack of access to extracurricular activities and basic computer literacy restricts opportunities for skill development and self-expression, preventing children from reaching their full potential[cite: 931].

03

Challenging Living Environments

Growing up in difficult surroundings makes children vulnerable to negative influences, increasing the risk of unhealthy and unproductive lifestyles[cite: 932].

Addressing these challenges holistically is essential to ensuring long-term growth, confidence, and success for every child[cite: 933].
